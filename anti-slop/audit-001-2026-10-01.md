# InviteStory UI audit

Audited 1 October 2026 against the running app at http://localhost:7100/.

The visual direction is strong: warm paper tones, expressive wedding typography, real invitation artwork, and distinctive envelope openings. The main problems are functional consistency, purchase information, and accessibility. Fix the high-priority findings before investing in further visual polish.

18 findings: 8 high priority, 8 medium priority, 2 low priority. Priorities reflect customer impact. Antislop rule references are supporting checklist references, not a claim of formal accessibility certification.

## Coverage and limits

All 137 local HTML pages received a browser DOM sweep at 390 × 844 and 1440 × 900. This checked document width, primary headings, and completed image failures. The inventory includes 125 blog pages, the storefront and alternate storefront, three policy pages, the confirmation page, two Dearly collection landing pages, and four local Dearly invitation demos.

Deeper visual and interaction checks covered the storefront, catalogue Grid/List/Match modes, INR/USD switching, search, preview viewer, preview FAQ, order sheet, express add-on, review lightbox, blog directory and representative articles, policy pages, confirmation page, and Dearly invitations. The preview selection sequence was cycled through all 35 designs; this does not certify every interaction inside every externally hosted invitation. All four local Dearly opening animations were started, and the Dahlia invitation received a deeper check of the opened state and conditional RSVP fields. The preview FAQ was also inspected at 320 × 568.

No payment was submitted. Razorpay's production checkout, payment cancellation/failure/success callbacks, and live receipt verification were not exercised. Bespoke and payment-success modal markup was inspected in source; those states were not forced open through injected application code. No WhatsApp messages, RSVP emails, or calendar events were sent. External DIY/reveal products were outside this local-app audit. No application code was changed.

The sweep found no document-level horizontal overflow at the two main test sizes. This is a limited geometry check, not a guarantee that every animated, lazy-loaded, or nested state is correct.

## High-priority findings

### 1. The linked Dearly collection page has broken branding and product images

**Where:** `/dearly/index.html`, reached through “Discover the Dearly experience” on the homepage. **Evidence:** the logo and all four envelope images fail. The logo becomes blue fallback text in the mobile header, crowding “All Designs.” The image paths resolve relative to `/dearly/` and point at the wrong locations. The separate `/dearly.html` page renders those assets correctly.

**Fix:** use one canonical collection page, correct its asset paths, and redirect or remove the duplicate. Validate both entry URLs and all four card images. **Checklist:** R-24, R-26, C-4.

![Broken Dearly header](/Users/amnas/Desktop/invitestory-main/anti-slop/ui-audit-2026-10-01/dearly-broken-mobile.jpg)

### 2. Switching from Match to Grid or List leaves the wrong catalogue content

**Reproduce:** choose Match, then Grid. The Grid button becomes selected and the grid container loses its Match class, but it still contains eight Match cards and zero normal template cards. Normal order buttons disappear. List has the same missing re-render path.

**Cause confirmed in source:** the Grid/List handlers change classes only; Match replaces the contents through `renderCatalogue()`. **Fix:** render the selected mode whenever it changes. Verify Grid → Match → Grid and List → Match → List with search and currency changes. **Checklist:** R-26, R-27, C-2.

### 3. Dearly has contradictory USD prices

**Reproduce:** choose USD. The homepage Dearly collection and its four featured cards display **$80**. The normal catalogue, preview, package card, and order sheet display **$75**. The dedicated collection pages also mix $80 display copy with $75 WhatsApp inquiry text.

**Impact:** customers cannot know which price applies before ordering. **Fix:** choose the intended USD price and derive every display and inquiry message from one pricing source. **Checklist:** R-38, C-5.

### 4. The order flow contradicts the refund policy about when details are required

**Where:** storefront, order sheet, confirmation page, and `/refund-and-editing-policy.html`. The storefront instructs customers to pay first and then send names, dates, photos, and venue. The policy says every customization requirement must be communicated in plain text **prior to payment**, and only pre-payment requirements are included in the first draft.

**Impact:** following the product's main instructions could violate its stated requirements. **Fix:** settle the actual operational sequence, align every page, and show the relevant conditions before checkout. This is a consistency finding, not legal advice. **Checklist:** R-38, C-5.

### 5. Order and review popups leave keyboard focus on the background

**Reproduce:** open an order sheet and press Tab. Focus moves from the original order button to the background preview button. Open a review lightbox and press Tab: focus moves to the next background review card. The order sheet also leaves background scrolling enabled: Page Down changed the underlying page's scroll position from 2493 to 3037 while the sheet remained open.

**Fix:** move focus into each dialog, contain keyboard navigation, make background content inert, lock background scrolling, and restore focus on dismissal. Check nested order/preview and FAQ states too. Preview itself correctly moves initial focus to its close button, but a full nested focus audit remains necessary. **Checklist:** R-32, C-4.

### 6. Desktop List view overlaps the price with the Preview button

**Where:** catalogue List view at 1440 × 900. In the first row, the price area spans x=863.5–951.5, while Preview spans x=887.2–1037.6. They occupy the same vertical band and overlap by approximately 64 px. This occurs with both INR and USD pricing.

**Fix:** allocate enough width for both buttons, prevent the action group from shrinking below its contents, and reflow controls at intermediate widths. **Checklist:** R-03, C-4.

![List layout overlap](/Users/amnas/Desktop/invitestory-main/anti-slop/ui-audit-2026-10-01/list-desktop.jpg)

### 7. Footer text becomes nearly invisible on policy and blog pages

**Evidence:** rendered footer paragraph color is `rgb(58,58,63)` over `rgb(13,10,4)` on the refund policy, approximately **1.75:1 contrast**. The terms footer and blog empty-result screenshot visibly show the same dark-on-dark problem. General paragraph styling overrides the footer's intended light text.

**Fix:** scope body-copy rules and explicitly set readable footer paragraph, link, and focus colors across all page families. **Checklist:** R-25, C-4.

![Low-contrast terms footer](/Users/amnas/Desktop/invitestory-main/anti-slop/ui-audit-2026-10-01/footer-contrast-mobile.jpg)

### 8. The confirmation page announces payment success without payment information

**Reproduce:** open `/thank-you.html` directly without query parameters. It displays a green checkmark, “Payment received!”, “Your slot is reserved,” and claims to be a receipt. Package, amount, and payment ID are all dashes. Its WhatsApp link still says the customer paid, using those missing values.

**Fix:** show a neutral “We couldn't find your payment details” state until a verified payment record is available. Provide a recovery action and distinguish confirmed, pending, and failed payments. This observation does not establish that the actual payment backend is insecure. **Checklist:** R-27, R-38, C-5.

## Medium-priority findings

### 9. The marketed Dearly RSVP experience exceeds what the local demo demonstrates

The package promises “Email RSVP with guest management dashboard.” The collection page describes form submissions that send email notifications directly. The inspected local RSVP implementation prepares a `mailto:` message and neither sends nor stores responses itself. All four demo host email values are empty; the demo explicitly tells guests email RSVP will become available when the host adds an address.

**Fix:** demonstrate the actual delivered workflow. If RSVP relies on the guest's email app, say that before purchase. If a dashboard exists elsewhere, show a working example and explain it. Its existence outside the audited files was not verified. **Checklist:** R-38, C-5.

### 10. Delivery timing changes between purchasing surfaces

Dearly's order sheet shows “24h Express Included” but its process still says “First Draft in 48h.” The featured trends article advertises instant 24-hour delivery starting at ₹1,999, while standard Premium pricing is 48 hours and express costs extra. The generic confirmation page also always says 48 hours.

**Fix:** derive delivery wording from the selected tier and add-on, and consistently state when the clock starts. **Checklist:** R-38, C-5.

### 11. The blog directory advertises 124 articles but exposes only 81

The heading and search placeholder promise 124 guides. The rendered directory has 81 article cards. There are 124 local article files, so **43 articles are absent from the searchable directory**. Representative article headers also still advertise “Browse 25 Templates” while the catalogue contains 35.

**Fix:** generate the directory and displayed counts from a shared inventory. Add the missing articles to search and browsing. **Checklist:** R-17, R-38, C-2.

### 12. Blog search has no empty-result feedback

**Reproduce:** search `zzzz-no-article` in the blog directory. The entire grid disappears, leaving blank space and the footer, with no message, result count, or reset action. The main template catalogue handles this better with “No templates found” and “Reset All Filters.”

**Fix:** add a query-specific empty state, clear-search action, and result count announced to assistive technology. **Checklist:** R-27, C-4.

![Blog search with no results](/Users/amnas/Desktop/invitestory-main/anti-slop/ui-audit-2026-10-01/blog-empty-mobile.jpg)

### 13. Mobile preview controls are too small for comfortable use

At 390 × 844, the tier tabs are about **17 px high**, 3D/Flat controls about **17 px high**, footer previous/next buttons **25 × 25 px**, and close **30 × 30 px**. Several labels are around 10–11 px. The title is heavily truncated because badges and actions share the same header row.

**Fix:** enlarge interactive hit areas, simplify secondary controls, and allow the design name its own line. Aim for comfortably tappable controls around 44 px without shrinking the invitation excessively. This is a usability finding; no formal target-size conformance assessment was performed. **Checklist:** R-03, R-32, C-4.

### 14. Dearly sample dates have already passed

All four local demos use **27 September 2026**, before this audit date. The opened Dahlia demo shows “Our celebration has begun” and a 00:00:00:00 countdown. The expired-state behavior works, but it weakens the live product demonstration.

**Fix:** use a future sample date or clearly present an intentional past-event example alongside a future countdown demo. **Checklist:** C-5, R-38.

### 15. Search fields rely on placeholder text instead of explicit labels

The storefront, alternate storefront, and blog search fields have no associated label, `aria-label`, or `aria-labelledby`. Their instructions disappear when a query is entered; the observed accessibility tree names the fields by their IDs rather than useful search instructions.

**Fix:** add explicit accessible labels such as “Search invitation designs” and “Search wedding guides.” Keep placeholders as optional examples. **Checklist:** R-32, C-4.

### 16. The order sheet buries the total and checkout action below lengthy content

On mobile, the initial order sheet viewport shows service positioning, the design summary, process steps, and included features; the total and checkout action require internal scrolling. The same long content pushes the primary action below the visible area on desktop too. Customers have already chosen a design but must traverse another sales explanation.

**Fix:** prioritize design, delivery choice, total, and checkout. Use a sticky summary/action area and concise or collapsible supporting information. Keep the close control reachable during scrolling. **Checklist:** R-05, C-3.

## Low-priority design improvements

### 17. The homepage repeats the purchase explanation

“Your invitation is 3 steps away,” “What happens after you order?”, pricing reassurance, the FAQ, and the order sheet repeat similar explanations. This length delays comparison and makes the overall page feel more repetitive than the distinctive invitation designs deserve.

**Fix:** keep one authoritative process explanation and use shorter contextual reassurance elsewhere. Preserve reviews and live previews, which provide stronger evidence than repeated promises. **Checklist:** R-05, C-3.

### 18. Navigation differs substantially across page families

The main header has currency and WhatsApp controls but no direct Catalogue/Packages navigation, and the logo is an image rather than a home link. Blog headers prioritize Bespoke, policies use separate navigation, and Dearly links jump to the production domain. On a long page, visitors must find section links in the hero or footer.

**Fix:** establish a small consistent navigation set, make the logo a home link, and keep same-site links relative where appropriate. Retain dedicated guest-invitation navigation for the invitation demos. **Checklist:** R-24, C-3.

## What worked in the inspected states

- The warm storefront palette, serif headline, and real invitation imagery establish a recognizable wedding brand.
- All 11 review images loaded while cycling the lightbox.
- Premium express correctly changed the order total from ₹1,999 to ₹2,498.
- Preview opens, moves initial focus to Close, and supports Escape dismissal. Escape closes the nested FAQ before the preview.
- The small-phone FAQ uses an internal scrolling body with a visible return action.
- Template search includes an empty state and reset control.
- Dearly openings expose a skip control; the opened demo offers motion and music controls and labels its fictional sample venue.
- Conditional RSVP guest-count fields appear when attendance is accepted.
- The inspected source includes reduced-motion handling. The system preference itself was not changed or fully tested.

## Recommended fix order

First fix findings **1–8**, which affect product visibility, ordering, trust, or basic access. Then align RSVP and delivery promises (**9–10**), repair blog discoverability and search (**11–12**), and improve mobile controls and ordering clarity (**13–16**). Apply the two structural refinements after the functional fixes.

Audit checks: page inventory and two-size DOM sweep **completed**; representative visual and popup checks **completed**; 35 preview selection transitions **exercised**; production payment lifecycle and complete external-demo interaction coverage **unverified**. The UI should not be treated as release-ready until the high-priority findings are resolved and those purchase states are tested.
